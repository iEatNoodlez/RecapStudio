    Video settings For this project. They apply the next time the video is made. The original text in speech bubbles and captions is painted out and the English lettered in its place, sized to fit the bubble; signs get a small English caption over them, and sound effects stay as drawn. Check the translation in the Script tab first: what's in the pictures is what it says there. The text is found by the same reader as Read text and painted over where it sits on a plain background, as in speech bubbles and caption boxes. Text drawn over the artwork (sound effects, signs) and bubbles with busy surroundings are left. The first video made this way reads every picture again (about as long as Read text); the cleaned pictures are then kept in the project's textless folder and reused. Each part gets its own subtitles and YouTube chapter list. YouTube takes up to 12 hours per video. Chapters already rendered are reused, so changing how the video is split, or re-rendering after editing a chapter, only makes what changed. Automatic scrolls webtoon chapters and pans and zooms other comics. Smaller sizes render much faster: 720p has under half the pixels of 1080p, 360p about a ninth.
    Background music
    Add a track for the music to play: without one the video is made without music.
    Your own tracks play in this order, then again from the first until the video ends, each evened out in loudness and fading into the next. Use music you're allowed to put on YouTube, such as tracks from the YouTube Audio Library, or the video may be claimed. It's added when the chapters are joined, so changing it only joins the video again, without drawing anything. The narration file (.wav) and Export for editors leave it out.
    Captions and Shorts The narration is drawn into the picture a few words at a time, the way CapCut's captions look, with the word being said picked out. Its timing comes from each line's sound, or from the speech recognizer when it's installed (Settings → Voice, for your own recordings), which is exact. The whole video is drawn again the next time it's made. Once the video is made, it's cut into parts that end in a pause between two lines, in the output folder's shorts folder: an 11-minute recap makes 13 one-minute Shorts. Portrait suits Shorts, TikTok and Reels best. YouTube takes Shorts of up to 3 minutes; TikTok and Reels longer.

    Title and thumbnail Your script writer reads the story and picks a clickbait title, a few others, one or two pictures for the thumbnail and what goes on them: yellow labels with arrows on the characters, a "?!" and a short speech bubble. The thumbnail is drawn at 1280×720 into the output folder. An image model repaints the thumbnail as one glossy illustration with the same characters and words, saved beside it as thumbnail_ai.jpg. It needs a service and its key in Settings → Thumbnail redraw, and costs a few cents each time.
    YouTube-safe Sex scenes are written the way an R-rated film shows them: kissing, undressing and that they had sex, said plainly, but no explicit acts, body detail or orgasms. Forced scenes are named once and told through what they leave behind. Black boxes cover nudity in the video. They come from the box finder (Settings → Video), from the script writer as it writes, and from you: click a piece and drag over what to cover. Changing this doesn't rewrite a script already written; redo the chapters to get the milder wording. This can make mistakes: boxes on things that don't need covering, a spot left uncovered, or wording that goes too far. Double-check all of it before you upload: watch the whole video and read the script.

        Panels
        
        
        
        A comic in Korean, Chinese or Japanese is read in that language, then your script writer translates it into English (the Translate step) before the script is written. Changing it applies when the text is read again.

        Characters
        
        
        
        The first 5 chapters is quicker: the main cast shows up early; every chapter finds everyone, and takes longer. Checking the names makes Make the recap wait for you; trusted ones are added as suggested.
        In a project that hasn't been scanned yet and has no named characters, Make the recap scans for the characters after reading the text, and your script writer suggests their names. Needs the character scan (the Characters tab).
      
    
      
    
        The scan group's pages
        
        Credits, Patreon and Discord notices, "read early" ads, other series and recruiting pages: found after Detect panels, and again after Read text, because they repeat across chapters or their text says so. They're unticked in the scene list (tick one to keep it) and cut out of the video.
        
        Checking shows one of each kind, and Make the recap waits for you; left out as found, it doesn't wait.

        Video
        Each project has its own format, size and style, chosen when it's made and changed in its Video tab.
        
        
    
          Subject finder (pan and zoom toward the subject)
          A project can pan and zoom toward each scene's main subject (its Video tab) instead of its middle. The main subject (usually the character) is found by U2-Net-p, a small open model (Apache 2.0): a 4.6 MB download into data\addons\focus, not part of the app. Each scene is looked at once (about 0.03 s on an NVIDIA GPU, 0.2 s on the CPU).
          
    
          
    
            
            
    
            
          
    
          
    
          
    
        
    
        
    
          Black boxes (YouTube-safe projects)
          The box finder covers exposed nipples and genitals. It's anime_censor_detection, an open model trained on drawn art (MIT, by deepghs): a 44.6 MB download into data\addons\boxes, not part of the app. Turn on YouTube-safe in a project's Video tab; rendering then looks at each piece used once (about 0.05 s on an NVIDIA GPU, 0.3 s on the CPU) before drawing the boxes.
          It makes mistakes: boxes on clothed bodies, faces or logos, and now and then a spot it misses. Look over every box before you upload.

        Cutting webtoons
        The bubble finder keeps a webtoon's cuts out of its speech bubbles, captions and sound effects. Without it a cut can pass between a bubble's outline and its text, leaving a sliver of the bubble at the bottom of one scene and the rest of it on the next (on test chapters, a quarter of the cuts did; with it, 7 in 1,885). It's comic-text-and-bubble-detector, an open model trained on manga, webtoons and western comics (Apache 2.0, by ogkalu): an 11.1 MB download into data\addons\bubbles, not part of the app. Detect panels takes longer with it, on the CPU: 10 to 40 seconds more a chapter.
        
    
        
    
          
          
    
          
        
    
        
    
          Downloaded: webtoon chapters are cut around their speech bubbles.
          Chapters already cut keep their scenes. Detect panels with Redo cuts a chapter again (its text, script and voice are then made again too).
          
        
    
        
    
      
    
      
    
        Plugins
        Plugins add features to Recap Studio. They only come from Recap Studio's own plugin list, made alongside the app, and run inside it. Installing, switching on or off and removing take effect the next time Recap Studio starts.

        Sources (getting chapters from sites)
        Recap Studio comes with no scrapers and no links to any. Bring your own: a plugin (a Python file for a site, from a file or a plugin list someone shares), gallery-dl, or a Suwayomi server with its extensions. Then a project's Chapters tab has Get chapters from a site.
        A plugin is a program: it runs on this PC with your permissions. Add only plugins from people you trust, and only get chapters you're allowed to.

    Finds the characters in your chapters, groups each one's appearances and suggests what they look like. You then name each group to add it to the character list, with pictures (used by Magi) and a description (used by the script writer).

    Export for video editors
    
    
    Saves the recap as an OpenTimelineIO timeline (.otioz) to finish in a video editor: a clip for each scene, the narration as a clip for each line, crossfades as transitions and a marker at each chapter. Its subtitles (.srt) are saved next to it, in the output folder.
    
    Made for
    
    
    
      
      Slide, Push, Blur dissolve and Smooth cut are Resolve's own transitions, with its default settings (change them in the Inspector). Other editors may show them as a plain dissolve or a cut.
    
    
    
    
    
    
    In Resolve, set the project's timeline resolution and frame rate to the video's () and keep image scaling on “Scale entire image to fit” (the default), then use File → Import → Timeline. In an editor that can't open .otioz, rename it to .zip, unzip it and open content.otio.
    
    
    
      
      
    
    
  
    
    



  
    
    
    Your own voice-over: 
    
    
    Record yourself reading this chapter's narration below, in order, as one file (WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC, OGG and others). Speech recognition on this computer (Vosk) splits it into the scenes' lines and checks each line against the script. Lines from your recording are used in the video instead of the generated voice, and Voice leaves them alone. Pauses between lines help it find where each one starts.
    
    
    The script 
    
    
    
    
    
      
    The speech recognizer is Vosk's small English model (Apache 2.0): a 41 MB download into data\addons\vosk, not part of the app.
